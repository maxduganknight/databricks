Executive Summary

Scenario SCEN-02 Accelerated Transition — assumptions revision. The carbon-price floor legislation has advanced, with the effective year pulled forward from 2032 to 2028 and the floor raised from $45/t to $85/t. Long-run natural gas demand growth is revised down by 1.4 percentage points per year (from +0.8%/yr to −0.6%/yr) on accelerating efficiency gains and electrification. Combined, these cut projected 2035–2045 cash flows, moving the scenario from an attractive NPV of ~$4.2B to a marginal ~$2.6B. Recommended client actions: hedge carbon exposure, re-time capex to the revised policy schedule, and revisit the assumed transition pace.

Impact on Projected Cash Flows

The revised carbon-price floor and the earlier policy-effective year raise carbon costs across the back half of the horizon, while the downgraded gas demand-growth curve erodes volumes in the 2035–2045 window. The two effects compound where the projection is most sensitive: the discounted cash flows in the final decade. The scenario's cumulative NPV therefore falls from an attractive ~$4.2B to a marginal ~$2.6B, with the divergence concentrated after 2034.

Recommended Client Discussion Options

  1. Hedge carbon exposure. With the floor at $85/t and policy effective from 2028, the scenario is materially more carbon-cost sensitive; consider forward carbon procurement or abatement investment to cap downside.
  2. Re-time capex to the revised policy schedule. Bring forward low-carbon capex and defer long-lived hydrocarbon commitments that only pay back beyond the revised policy horizon.
  3. Revisit the assumed transition pace. Test an intermediate pace between Accelerated Transition and the Base Case; the marginal NPV suggests the aggressive-transition thesis is no longer clearly value-accretive under the new assumptions.
